Every species can make more offspring than its surroundings can support. Step through Darwin's argument, run the numbers yourself, then check what you learned.

Step 1 · The idea

Even slow breeders outrun their food

Charles Darwin saw that every species can produce more young than its environment can feed. He picked African elephants as his test case because they are among the slowest breeders on Earth. A female bears about six calves over roughly 90 years of fertility, and about half of them are female. Even at that pace, one pair's descendants would fill a continent within a few centuries if nothing held them back.

Step 2 · Simulation

How long until a population outweighs Earth?

Unrestricted growth is hard to picture, so this simulation gives it a finish line: the point where the whole population weighs as much as Earth, 6 × 1024 kg (6 septillion kg). Every run starts with one pair.

A population's growth rate, written r, depends on births and deaths over time. With plenty of food and space it reaches its maximum, rmax. Pick an organism and an rmax, then run it. You can plot up to five comparisons.

    Putting it together

    Populations can grow exponentially, but food, space and other resources cannot. So far more individuals are born than can survive, and some will do better than others. Darwin saw that this struggle is what makes natural selection possible.
